## Common rules
- Grid: EPSG:32645, 10 m, the Sentinel-2 composite grid (2895 x 2424). Study mask: BMC municipal polygon (166.5 sq km). Wards raster 1-67.
- Every task: tests written first and seen failing; implementation; full suite twice; interface check against the next task's needs; then commit + doc + memory.
- Randomness: fixed seeds; stochastic results reported over repeated runs.
- Memory: 8 GB machine; memory_guard on every long run; no two heavy jobs at once.
- Honest labelling: every number in the chapter must trace to an artefact in outputs/ produced by a committed script.

## Tasks
### T0 grid and study mask
Interface: src.grid.load_grid() -> (transform, shape, crs); study_mask(); ward_raster(); read_aligned(path, resampling).
Tests: shape equals the S2 composite; mask area within 1 percent of 166.5 sq km; all 67 wards present, no pixel in two wards; DEM read_aligned returns grid shape.
### T5 vegetation health over time (index-based, NOT deep learning)
Method: NDVI from 8 Nov-Mar composites (2018-19 .. 2025-26), per-pixel least-squares slope + Mann-Kendall style significance, latest-year z-score anomaly versus the previous 7 years, ward and land-cover summaries.
Tests: synthetic stack recovers a known slope; NaN handling; anomaly sign; real data: NDVI in [-1,1], class ordering trees > crops > built > water.
Limitation: composites use the 5 clearest days per season.
RESULT (2026-10-21): (1) The raw yearly NDVI is NOT comparable: unchanged land (same Esri class 2017-2023) shifts together by -0.044..+0.057 between years in every class, and acquisition date explains none of it (R2 0.01). A common additive year offset is estimated on unchanged non-water land and removed (std of city median 0.045 -> 0.011; residual class-specific shifts of about 0.02-0.03 remain, e.g. stable cropland drifts +0.0056/yr). (2) The ORIGINAL pre-written check (all vegetation -> built-up must trend down more than stable built-up) FAILED, and the threshold was not loosened. Cause: November-March cropland and scrub are already bare/low-NDVI, so crop->built leaves almost no signal (0.386 -> 0.372, median slope +0.0004/yr). Tree->built does: 0.505 -> 0.452, median slope -0.0057/yr vs -0.0006/yr for stable built-up (Mann-Whitney p 3e-202, P(converted slope above stable) about 0.40). Test rewritten to the supported claim only. (3) Per-pixel significance is weak (9.7 percent of tree->built pixels significant vs 6.7 percent of stable built); use ward-level aggregates, not pixel maps, in the chapter. The p-values are not corrected for multiple testing or spatial autocorrelation.
### T4 land-use model (U-Net, PyTorch MPS)
Inputs: S2 2023 composite (10 bands, offset-corrected, per-band standardised). Weak labels: Esri/IO LULC 2023 collapsed to the 6 chapter classes (Water, Trees, Cropland, Grass/Scrub, Built-up, Bare ground).
Split: spatial blocks (no patch leakage). Loss: class-weighted cross-entropy. Seeds fixed; train twice.
Tests: label mapping total; patches finite and correct shape; block split disjoint; model forward shape; tiny-batch overfit reaches > 95 percent; tiled inference reconstructs the full map without seams.
Evidence: spatial-validation agreement with the weak labels (agreement, NOT accuracy). Real accuracy only from the authors' 300 hand labels, compared for BOTH our map and the Esri map.
Limitation: the model learns another model's labels; any claim of accuracy waits for T7.
RESULT (2026-10-21), agreement with the weak Esri labels on held-out 512 px blocks, NOT accuracy: U-Net seed 0 overall 84.74 percent, macro-F1 0.745; seed 1 84.73 percent, macro-F1 0.754 (training 91.9 / 92.1 percent). Majority-class baseline 41.5 percent. Per-pixel LightGBM baseline on the same bands and labels: 70.0 percent, macro-F1 0.628, so spatial context adds about 15 points. Per class (seed 0, F1): built-up 0.93, trees 0.88, cropland 0.86, water 0.79, grass/scrub 0.54, bare ground 0.47 (precision 0.31, only 4,576 held-out pixels). Grass/scrub is confused mainly with cropland and trees; water pixels are sometimes called bare. Seeds agree on 94.4 percent of BMC pixels, but only 49.9 percent of pixels either seed calls grass/scrub (built-up 96.6, water 85.3, bare 85.0, trees 80.4, cropland 73.1). Class shares inside BMC differ between seeds by about 1.5 points for cropland (12.4 vs 10.9) and grass/scrub (5.9 vs 7.3); built-up 73.4 vs 73.6. Primary map: seed 0 (declared before running); report seed spread for crop/grass areas. 2023 composite -> 2025-26 composite (same training statistics): built-up rises 0.6-0.9 points (no collapse) but grass/scrub falls about 2 points in both seeds, probably seasonal (Nov-Feb vs Jan-Apr + Oct-Nov scenes), so do not read 2023 -> 2025 grass/crop changes as land change.
INCIDENT: the first seed-0 run crashed (SIGSEGV in libomp) when LightGBM started after PyTorch in one process (two OpenMP runtimes). Fixed by process isolation: src/landuse.py imports torch lazily, the baseline lives in src/landuse_baseline.py and runs separately; two regression tests prove neither module drags torch into the baseline process (both failed on the old code). The U-Net weights had been saved before the crash, so seed 0 was evaluated from the saved weights (identical numbers to the training log).
### T6 flood-prone zones
Labels: Sentinel-1 monsoon 2025 water extent (VV dB, speckle filtered, automatic threshold) minus permanent water (dry-season reference), per-pixel flood frequency; scenes chosen after heavy-rain days when ERA5 supports it.
Features: elevation, slope, HAND, TWI, curvature, log flow accumulation, distance to mapped drains/canals/river/water, WorldCover built-up fraction, median NDVI.
Model: gradient boosting with spatially blocked cross-validation. Check: percentile rank of the 19 hotspot points inside BMC, versus elevation-only and HAND-only baselines and a random baseline, with bootstrap intervals.
DATA FACT (checked 2026-10-21, data/interim/s1_inventory.csv): of 33 monsoon scenes only 25 cover more than 95 percent of the study area (8 cover under 50 percent, edge-of-swath); of 26 dry scenes 18 are usable (1 failed download). Use only full-coverage scenes. Heaviest ERA5 day (3 Sep, 72 mm) has no scene within 3 days; usable scenes with more than 15 mm the day before: 27 Jun, 24 Jul, 26 Aug (24.4 mm). Scenes alternate in 3-day pairs (two orbits), so thresholds are set per scene.
KNOWN GAP: SAR cannot see street flooding in dense built-up areas (double-bounce); labels mostly capture open-area inundation. The hotspot check is therefore the independent test, and the limitation goes in the chapter.
Tests: synthetic DEM with known HAND; threshold function on a synthetic bimodal image; permanent-water exclusion; spatial CV folds disjoint; hotspot-rank function on toy data.

## T6 protocol (registered 2026-10-21, BEFORE any flood result was computed)
Evidence used to fix the design (labels/reference only, never the hotspots): scenes come from two Sentinel-1A tracks, relative orbit 85 (ascending) and 121 (descending) (data/interim/s1_orbits.csv): usable monsoon scenes 13 + 12 = 25, usable dry scenes 8 + 10 = 18. Open water versus vegetated land (WorldCover 2021) separates only moderately on raw VV (balanced accuracy 0.81-0.92); a 3x3 median filter on dB adds 2-3 points (5x5 adds about 4 but would erase narrow drains); best thresholds sit at -10 to -13 dB. Therefore:
1. T6a flood evidence per monsoon scene: VV in dB, 3x3 median filter, scene threshold t = argmax Youden J between permanent-water and vegetated-land reference pixels (scene excluded if balanced accuracy < 0.80); change detection against the median filtered dB of the SAME orbit's dry scenes: flood = (x < t) and (x - reference <= -3 dB, the conventional change threshold; literature support to be verified before it is cited) and not persistently dark (water-like in at least 50 percent of that orbit's dry scenes; removes runways, asphalt, permanent water) and slope < 5 degrees and HAND < 20 m.
2. T6b terrain at native 30 m: filled DEM, D8 flow direction, flow accumulation, slope, curvature, TWI, HAND relative to channels = mapped drains/canals/river (ward map) united with accumulation cells above a threshold; distances to drains, canals, river, water bodies. DEM is a surface model (buildings, trees included): local depressions in the dense city are unreliable.
3. T6c labels: per-pixel flood frequency F over valid monsoon scenes. Built-up is EXCLUDED from model training (SAR cannot see street flooding: double-bounce) using WorldCover 2021 built-up united with the U-Net 2025-26 built-up map, dilated 2 px, and permanent water is excluded. Built-up share is NOT a model feature (the SAR labels would teach it that built-up never floods). The positive threshold tau is set from the distribution of F alone and written here BEFORE any model is fitted.
4. T6d model: LightGBM binary classifier on terrain + distance features only, 5-fold spatial block cross-validation (2 km blocks), AUC and average precision per fold, single-feature baselines (HAND, elevation, TWI, distance to drain); final model fitted on all blocks and applied to every pixel in BMC. LightGBM runs in its own process (no torch).
5. T6e hotspot test, ONE SHOT after the model is frozen (no tuning against hotspots): for each of the 16 sanitation-plan hotspots take the maximum susceptibility percentile within 150 m of its point(s) (WL8 and WL11 have several points), percentile computed over all BMC pixels excluding permanent water. Statistic: mean percentile over the 16. Null: 5000 random sets of 16 pixels with the same aggregation; one-sided permutation p. Same statistic for the baselines (low elevation, low HAND, high TWI, near drain). PASS if model mean percentile >= 70 and p < 0.05. If it fails, that is reported as a failure of the model, not repaired.
T6b decision (2026-10-21, before any flood result): stream threshold = 300 contributing cells (0.27 sq km). Basis: DEM-derived streams vs mapped drains/canals/river/OSM waterways inside BMC: 38 percent of mapped-channel cells lie within 1 cell of a derived stream at 300 cells (51 percent at 50, 32 percent at 1000), and only 35 percent of derived-stream cells lie within 2 cells of a mapped channel (a surface-model DEM cannot see underground city drains). Sensitivity to the threshold (100/300/1000) is reported with the model. pysheds 0.5 needs a NumPy-2 shim for np.in1d (tests/test_terrain.py). HAND is undefined on the 1-cell tile border; BMC lies at least 93 cells inside the tile. Built: 4 of 184,887 BMC cells had HAND -9e-5 m (float32 round-off in pysheds); clipped to 0 with a guard that raises if anything below -0.01 m. HAND is NaN for 0.07 percent of BMC cells (no drainage path found); LightGBM takes NaN natively. Band order in terrain_features.tif: elevation, slope_deg, laplacian, log10_acc_m2, twi, hand, dist_drain, dist_river, dist_water, dist_channel; channels.tif: mapped_drains, river, derived_acc_ge_300 (BMC channel cells: 16,901 mapped-drain, 19,964 river, 29,326 derived in the whole tile).
T6c RESULT AND LABEL DECISION (2026-10-21, recorded BEFORE any model was fitted):
- All 43 scenes passed the quality rule (balanced accuracy 0.82-0.94; monsoon 13 + 12 used, dry 8 + 10). Flagged surface water per scene: 0.4-1.9 percent of open (non-built, non-water) BMC pixels; highest on 27 Jun and 24 Jul (the rain-matched scenes), lowest in Sep-Oct. Flood-frequency F (25 scenes): 90th percentile 0.04; F >= 0.12 (3 or more of 25 scenes) in 2.41 percent of open pixels (23,838 px); only 0.1 percent of built-up pixels reach F >= 0.10 (radar blind there, as expected).
- FINDING (outputs/flood/rain_calendar_diagnostic.csv, pinned by a test): the flagged area follows the CALENDAR, not the rain. Cropland: 2.93 percent of pixels flagged in June-August scenes vs 0.67 percent in Sep-Oct (4.4 times), Spearman with day of year -0.67 (p < 0.001), with antecedent 3-day / 7-day rain +0.14 / -0.08 (no response). Grass: calendar -0.55 (p 0.004), no rain response. Trees and shrub: slightly NEGATIVE with 7-day rain (-0.49, -0.47; wet canopies scatter more). Only bare ground rises with 7-day rain (+0.40, p 0.049). 81.3 percent of the positives are cropland, consistent with managed paddy-field water. The radar label is therefore NOT a measure of rain-driven flooding.
- DECISION: the label is described everywhere as MONSOON SURFACE-WATER FREQUENCY on open land (an inundation propensity), never as flood extent. Positive class: F >= 0.12 (>= 3 of 25 scenes; chosen from the F distribution alone, to avoid single-scene noise). Cropland is kept in training because paddy lowlands are low, flat and near drains (the terrain controls are still informative), but model performance is also reported separately within cropland and within non-cropland (diagnostic only). The sanitation-plan hotspot test (T6e) stays the arbiter of whether the susceptibility is useful for street-level waterlogging.
- T6d training unit (registered): 30 m cells on the DEM grid; y = 1 if at least 30 percent of the cell's open 10 m pixels have F >= 0.12; a cell enters training only if at least 50 percent of its 10 m pixels are open (not built per WorldCover 2021 united with the U-Net 2025-26 built map, dilated 2 px; not permanent water). Built-up share is not a feature.
T6d RESULT, model FROZEN 2026-10-21 before the hotspot test: LightGBM (400 trees, 31 leaves, lr 0.05, seed 0; hyperparameters fixed in advance, no tuning), 10 terrain/hydrology features, 380,142 eligible 30 m cells, 5-fold spatial block CV (2 km blocks). Pooled out-of-fold AUC 0.869 (folds 0.841-0.889), average precision 0.576 against prevalence 0.217. Single-feature AUCs (physical direction): elevation 0.817, slope 0.802, HAND 0.742, TWI 0.713, distance to river 0.681, water 0.596, drain 0.594. Within groups: cropland 0.785, non-cropland 0.895, inside BMC 0.845, outside BMC 0.867. Stream-threshold sensitivity (100/300/1000 cells): CV AUC 0.867/0.869/0.869, so the threshold does not drive the result. Feature importance (gain): elevation, slope, HAND, distance to river, distance to water dominate.
DEPARTURE FROM THE REGISTERED LABEL RATIONALE, disclosed: tau was read from the BMC distribution (2.4 percent of open pixels), but training uses the whole 700 sq km tile, which is mostly peri-urban and rural paddy land: 21.7 percent of eligible cells are positive (23.4 percent outside BMC, 7.3 percent inside; 2,861 positive cells inside BMC) and 95.5 percent of positives are cropland. The model therefore mostly learns where open land holds monsoon surface water (low, flat land near rivers); elevation is its strongest single feature, so regional position (coastal plain versus western upland) carries much of the signal. Applied to the dense city it is an extrapolation, tested only by T6e.
Known limits added: no rain response in the radar label (paddy water confounds it; only 3 rain-matched scenes, and the 3 Sep 72 mm event has no scene within 3 days).
PROTOCOL AMENDMENT (2026-10-21, before the real hotspot test was run; found while checking the null with synthetic scores): step 5 above specified the MAXIMUM percentile within 150 m. A 150 m disk holds about 81 cells, so on a random score that statistic averages 98.6 percentile (95 percent of disk maxima are above the 95th percentile) and the bar 'mean percentile >= 70' would be passed by noise. The aggregator is replaced by the MEAN percentile within the 150 m disk (random score -> 50.0 +- 0.3), still taking the maximum over a hotspot's points (WL8, WL11 have several; the null gives each hotspot the same number of random points). Bar (>= 70), p < 0.05, the 5000-set null and the baselines are unchanged. Nothing about the model changed (it was frozen and committed first).
Known limits: hotspot positions are locality-level (200-400 m); SAR labels see open-area inundation only; 25 scenes of one monsoon (2025); rainfall-matched scenes are few (27 Jun, 24 Jul, 26 Aug).

### T7 accuracy estimator (code now, run when labels arrive)
Stratified estimator (Olofsson 2014, Stehman 2014): overall, user's and producer's accuracy with standard errors, from strata weights in validation/strata_weights.csv. Tests: known confusion matrices from hand calculation; labelled-vs-unsure handling; blind-labelling guard.
### T8 graph twin
Nodes: connected patches (water, green = trees + grass/scrub, farm = cropland) above a minimum area; attributes: area, class, ward, yearly land-cover shares 2017-2023, NDVI by year, flood susceptibility, GBIF records/species within a buffer. Edges: adjacency, habitat connectivity within a dispersal distance (weighted), flow links along drainage.
Tests: node count equals labelled components; edge symmetry where required; attributes complete; GBIF assigned once.
### T9 scenario engine + SDG-aligned proxies
Scenarios: wetland restoration, community gardens, tree corridors, as explicit edits to the land-use map and graph. Metrics: SCS curve-number runoff (uncalibrated; soil group assumed), connectivity (probability of connectivity), food-potential index (cropland area, NOT a yield forecast), public open space share (SDG 11.7.1 proxy from OSM green/open space).
Tests: baseline reproduces itself; each scenario changes only its intended cells; metrics move in the expected direction on toy graphs.
### T10 NSGA-II (pymoo)
Decision: choose intervention sites under an area budget. Objectives: reduce flood exposure, raise food-potential index, raise connectivity. Output: Pareto front, hypervolume, repeated seeds.
Tests: constraint handling; known toy Pareto front recovered; seeds reproduce.
### T11 transfer test (bonus)
Second, less-instrumented city (candidate: Cuttack). Same scripts with a new bbox; needs fresh downloads at about 1 MB/s. Do only if T0-T10 are done and verified.

## Known limitations to carry into the chapter
Weak labels (Esri) for T4; SAR blind to urban street flooding (T6); scene-selection noise (T5); CN soil assumption (T9); GBIF 98 percent birds and 71 percent from 2021+ (T8); hotspot positions locality-level (T6 check); no sensor data (Aurassure dropped); 2021 S2 composite is Jan-Apr only.
